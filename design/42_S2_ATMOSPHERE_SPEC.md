# 42 - S2: cover lighting and atmosphere in the real game (art director spec)

Target: `art_src/reference/cover.jpg` and `docs/41_COVER_DIRECTION.md` section "What the cover actually is". The user's words: the cover shows a vision the real game should reach; most of its appeal is light, colour, depth and life, not model detail. Everything must be real, in-engine (Godot 4.7, gl_compatibility renderer, web build must still work; use `perf_governor` levels to switch heavy parts off).

Existing hooks: `game/scripts/world/day_cycle.gd` (sun energy/colour, fog, ambient, glow, saturation, driven by time of day and progress), `sky.gd`, `shaders/sea.gdshader`, `shaders/ground.gdshader`, `shaders/soft_toon.gdshader`, `world_builder.gd` (_environment, lamp, _distant), `systems/perf_governor.gd`. Keep the time-of-day system and the progress-driven mood. Do not change gameplay.

## What to achieve (in priority order)
1. **Colour script per time of day** (morning, afternoon, golden hour, dusk, and the existing special states): warm key light (orange-gold at low sun), COOL lavender/blue ambient and shadows (warm lit sides vs cool shadow sides is the core of the cover), sky gradient purple -> pink -> orange toward the sun at dusk with pink-lit clouds; in the day a bright clean warm-blue sky. Colours must stay inside a tasteful palette; avoid clipping and washed-out white.
2. **Glowing lights at dusk/night** (the cover's biggest magic): warm emissive windows on houses/shops, round glowing bulbs on lamp posts along the pier and streets, string lights/bunting bulbs on the pier and cafe, lit Ferris wheel lights, lighthouse lamp. They fade in as the sun lowers and out in the day (driven from day_cycle), implemented cheaply (emissive vertex colour / emission energy via the shared materials, a handful of shared materials, no per-light OmniLight3D except at most 2-3 near the player if the budget allows). Use glow (bloom) so they bloom softly. Verify bloom actually works in gl_compatibility on this engine and web; if not, fake it with additive billboard sprites/quads (batched via MultiMesh).
3. **Water**: teal-blue with depth gradient (lighter near shore), at dusk lavender/pink horizon reflections, a **sun-glitter path** toward the sun (sparkles on the waves), soft white foam lines along shores and piers, calm stylised waves. No glaring white/cyan plane.
4. **Depth and haze**: atmospheric perspective (distant land lighter, bluer/pinker, lower contrast) using fog colour tied to the sky horizon; layered soft hills and a distant lighthouse island with wind turbines on the horizon like the cover. Replace the harsh giant cone mountains in the far field with soft, irregular, hazy silhouettes (decor only, collision/terrain untouched).
5. **Shading**: sun-facing rim light on the gull and people (warm), shadows soft and cool-tinted; lighten the gull's wings (cover wings are white/pale lilac-grey, not mid-grey; keep dark tips) and keep the gull clean white at all times of day.
6. **No over-saturation by progress**: the existing progress-driven glow/saturation boost must plateau (cap) so late-game images never blow out or lose information; design the change as deliberate colour states rather than ever-increasing intensity.
7. **Clouds**: soft stylised cumulus lit pink/orange at dusk (existing cloud_drift), keep cheap.

## Performance
Budget: no more than +10 % frame time on the same hardware/scene (report A/B medians on the same camera, plus draw calls); heavy parts (glow, glitter, extra layers) off at `perf_governor` levels 2-3 and in web lite mode. Emissive/glow additions must not add per-object scripts.
